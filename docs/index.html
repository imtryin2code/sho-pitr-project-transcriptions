The 1941 Chinook Jargon Field Transcriptions of Joe Peter and Jack Marr

History & The Recording Equipment

In the early 1940s, legendary linguist John Peabody Harrington tasked his assistant, 16-year-old Jack Marr, with capturing the voices of Indigenous speakers along the West Coast. While Harrington sought "Pure Chinook," Marr unknowingly documented 10 hours of a high-level variety of Chinook Jargon (Chinuk Wawa) from Joe Peter.

The Presto Recorder

Marr utilized a Presto portable aluminum disc recorder (likely a Model Y). This high-end field unit weighed approximately 150 pounds and required massive storage batteries and power inverters to operate in remote locations without electricity.

The Aluminum Discs

Grooves were cut directly into 12-inch bare aluminum discs using a sapphire needle. These were pure metal—durable for travel, but susceptible to high surface noise, requiring expert digitization by the Smithsonian to be heard today.


Technical Note: These recordings survive as roughly 15 double-sided discs. Despite the primitive recording conditions, Marr’s work preserved a vital linguistic link to the Cowlitz and Chinook ancestry of Joe Peter.
